# PDF specification — Filmmic Foundations

Reference example: 16-page *Mindglass Procedure: Memory, Grief, and the Repetition of Avoidance*. See Publication Standard §6 for measured values.

## Design and production
- US Letter (612 × 792 pt) is an established baseline, not an inviolable rule.
- Restrained academic typographic hierarchy, uncluttered light paper, deep-blue ink, muted teal separators, concise running heads and page numbers.
- Main body text should prioritize comfortable readability (~10–11pt preferred where feasible). Existing sample uses approximately 8.9pt for dense text; do not blindly clone that small size.
- Consistent paragraph rhythm, no orphan headings, clear section hierarchy. Use the paper's actual argument to determine section breaks and pacing.
- Professional cover: title, film, essay subtitle, accurate verified film still and caption, anonymous organizational author. Multi-page papers usually include a table of contents and abstract/scope.
- Clearly differentiated film stills vs original analytical diagrams, numbered figures with factual captions. Place illustrations near relevant text. Preserve original aspect ratio.
- References: original film as primary source, other research with verified citation details. Every sourced assertion must be traceable; bibliography does not prove content.
- Font embedding or accessible equivalent; selectable text, active links, logical reading order; publication author metadata Filmmic Foundations only. Scan for identifying metadata.
- Render every page to image, manually inspect, compare edits; verify full page count, margins, no clipped captions or broken links.

## Writing and interpretation
- Film-specific research question and nuanced central claim.
- Scene-level observation plus filmmaking technique plus argued meaning plus evidence/qualification.
- Real-world sources provide conceptual context; do not use science to diagnose fictional people or claim invented technologies are established practice.
- Treat ambiguity as ambiguity, include alternative readings and limitations where relevant.

## Future PDF authoring workflow
1. Verify source film, scenes and reference bibliography.
2. Draft with explicit figure placements, labels and full references.
3. Lay out publication-consistent paper.
4. Embed fonts, links and neutral document metadata; remove accidental creator identity.
5. Render to images, inspect and revise all pages.
6. Test links, selectable text and accessibility properties.


## Figure and format parity — new requirement
- Match the established Mindglass cover sequence: **title/subtitle above a credited film still**, then a restrained scope summary; avoid replacing it with a full-bleed poster page.
- A new article PDF must contain the **entire long-form article**, not an abridged subset. Compare heading-by-heading with the HTML after image insertion and verify references were retained.
- Figure numbers, still identities, sequence relevance and diagrams should agree between the page and PDF; the same local full-frame stills, no duplicates or thumbnail enlargements. Original web HTML diagrams can be exported to print-resolution images for PDF only.
- Keep table of contents, consistent main text size, section heads, running heads, page numbering, margins and source links; avoid a new template per article. Use *Mindglass* as an editorial reference and *Alike* as a reminder that adding images must not reset formatting.
- Test PDF URL in article action menu and hero; render **all pages** and check clipping, stray blank pages and line-break handling in captions. Make metadata organization-only.

### Cover and preview consistency (October 2026 revision)

The Mindglass Procedure PDF is the visual baseline. Use a sans-serif, title-first cover with a horizontal rule; an authentic still captioned as such; a compact article summary and 2-column film facts; a separate contents page; consistent section type, figure numbering, running headers and linked references. Place real stills near the paragraphs they support. Never substitute analytical artwork and label it "film still." If local film frames are unavailable, retain honest interpretive labels and mark the PDF as media-incomplete.

### Contents and cover asset rules (October 23, 2026)
- The *Mindglass Procedure* PDF is authoritative for the first-page sequence: left-aligned title, subtitle, teal rule, authentic captioned film still, concise "An in-depth critical analysis" blurb, and compact film-information table. Compare every new cover to the source, not just another article.
- The contents page is **plain text**, not a table of clickable in-PDF links. Preserve clickable external references later in the paper; never remove all PDF annotations indiscriminately.
- Use a lossless film frame as the master when available. Derive visually identical, high-quality JPEG assets and responsive variants; do not replace existing shots with unrelated frames simply to increase resolution. Verify exact shot match before substitution.
- If direct access to a hosting service's video stream is unavailable, report that limitation. Do not invent "high-resolution" film stills, substitute generated frames, or upscale a small screenshot and call it additional detail.
