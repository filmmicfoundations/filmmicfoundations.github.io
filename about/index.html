01 / PURPOSE

A resource for examining short films.

Filmmic Foundations publishes educational research and critical readings of short cinema. Articles explore choices of framing, editing, sound, production design, narrative structure, genre, and symbolism, drawing on the films themselves and relevant external research when available.

The archive is not a rating platform or promotional portfolio. Its goal is to make careful film interpretation accessible, inspectable, and useful for further study.

02 / EDITORIAL STANDARDS

Evidence before preference.

  • Separate observation from interpretation. Identify what happens on screen before arguing what it might mean.
  • Represent reasonable alternatives. Where film language supports more than one reading, acknowledge the uncertainty instead of presenting a theory as established fact.
  • Distinguish facts from value judgments. Technical details, dates, credits, and claims about historical context should be supported by verifiable sources.
  • Discuss films respectfully. Critique formal choices and ideas without asserting intent, motive, or private experience without evidence.
  • Disclose relevant limitations. A lack of primary documentation, access to production information, or potentially relevant production relationships should not be concealed when it affects interpretation.

Absolute neutrality is not achievable in interpretation. The archive aims to minimize unexamined bias through transparent reasoning, sources, and careful qualifications.

03 / SOURCES

Traceable research.

Films are treated as primary texts. Articles distinguish close analysis of an image or sequence from research claims supported by books, scholarly work, interviews, archival material, or reliable institutional publications.

References appear within each article. The Cite this article tool formats citations to the archive publication; it does not substitute for reviewing the article's own source list. Citation formats are conveniences and should be checked against the relevant style guide.

04 / INTERPRETATION

Critical, not definitive.

An interpretation is an argument supported by evidence, not the final word about a film's meaning. A subject such as grief, trauma, or mental health requires additional care: fictional depictions are not diagnoses, and psychological research should not be presented as proving a character's condition or a filmmaker's intentions.

05 / CORRECTIONS & UPDATES

Changes should be visible.

Research can be revised when better documentation or a substantiated correction becomes available. Article headers provide publication and update dates, where applicable. Material corrections should be noted in the article, with an explanation of what changed. Quiet edits should not alter the substance of an argument.

New and updated articles appear in the archive's RSS feed.

06 / ANONYMITY & PRIVACY

Publication-level attribution.

Research is published under the organizational name Filmmic Foundations, without personal researcher bylines or public contributor profiles. Film credits and attributed source authors may be included when necessary for factual accuracy; those credits are not claims about who manages the archive.

The website does not include user accounts, advertising trackers, or a comment system. GitHub Pages hosts the site; embedded YouTube videos and remotely served font files may involve requests to their respective services. The public GitHub repository and its commit history can also reveal account names or commit metadata independently of the website itself.
